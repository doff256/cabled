# R8: framing settings, camera controls, quality pins, thermal

This run closes P1.

## What was built

- **Framing:** a "show more ↔ show less" slider, a speed setting (Calm, Normal or Fast) and an "Everyone" option that frames every face. Faces narrower than 40% of the biggest face are ignored.
- **Quicker defaults:** the springs are about 1.7× faster, the dead zone shrank from 0.08 to 0.05, and the framer waits 1.5 s instead of 2 s before reacting.
- **Box in a box:** the side panel shows the whole camera view, with the part being sent outlined.
- **Camera controls:**
  - Focus: auto, or manual lens position.
  - Exposure: auto with a brightness bias, or manual ISO and shutter. The shutter can't be longer than one frame.
  - White balance: auto, or manual temperature and tint.
  - Click the preview to focus and expose at that point. The point is mapped back through the crop and the rotation.
  - Settings are kept when you switch cameras.
- **Quality pins (R-58):** you can fix the capture resolution (720p, 1080p or 4K) and frame rate (30, 60 or 120). Auto keeps the old behaviour.
- **Thermal step-down:** when the phone reports "serious", capture is capped at 1080p30. At "critical" it's capped at 720p30.
- **Side panel:** shows on the right while the phone is connected.
- **iPhone build:** 9. (Build 7 crashed about 15 s after connecting: the live white-balance gains were briefly out of range, and converting them to temperature/tint raises an Objective-C exception. Build 9 clamps the gains first; build 8 also keeps NaN out of the JSON.) The virtual camera DLL is unchanged, so you don't need to reinstall it.

## Owner test checklist (iPhone build 10 + new Cabled.exe)

1. Open Cabled.exe and connect the phone. The side panel should appear and the box-in-box should show the outline moving.
2. Drag "show more ↔ show less" both ways. The crop should widen and tighten.
3. Try Calm and Fast. Fast should follow you noticeably quicker. Check that Normal now feels quick enough.
4. With two people in shot, tick Everyone. Both should stay in frame.
5. Click on something in the preview. Focus and exposure should move there.
6. Switch focus, exposure and white balance to Manual and drag their sliders. The picture should change. Switch them back to Auto.
7. Set Capture to 1080p and Frame rate to 60, and check the stats line. Then set both back to Auto.
8. Optional: let the phone get warm. The log should show the step-down.

## Results

### Build 9 (2026-11-21)

Build 9 no longer crashed, but the owner reported four problems:

1. **Switching resolution took long and looked like it didn't work.**
   - The iPhone rebuilt its whole camera session on every change, and the framer restarted from the full view.
   - Fix: a fast path changes the format on the running session. The framer now carries the crop across a size change.
2. **The framing slider sometimes worked, sometimes didn't.**
   - The quality guard (no upscaling) left no room to zoom whenever capture equalled output size, for example a pinned 1080p.
   - Fix: up to 1.5× upscaling is now allowed. The panel shows the zoom and says when the capture size limits it.
3. **Auto "show more/less" defeated the slider.**
   - The framer resized the crop to the face size, and zoomed out to the full view when the face was lost.
   - Fix: the slider is now a fixed zoom, from 1× (the whole view) to 3×. The framer only pans and tilts, and widens only when "Everyone" needs more room. When nobody is in shot it stays where it was.
4. **Clicking the preview did nothing.**
   - With a face in view, iOS's face-driven focus and exposure overrode the clicked point.
   - Fix: clicking turns face-driven focus and exposure off. Choosing Auto again turns them back on. A yellow square shows where you clicked, and the PC log records the point.
